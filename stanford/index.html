1 · The problem

Transport an atom cold between two traps—fast

$$ D,\; T,\; F = |c_0(T)|^2 $$
  • Distance \(D\) — how far the trap moves (3–200 μm)
  • Time \(T\) — how long we allow (goal: minimum)
  • Fidelity \(F\) — ground-state population at arrival
  • Find minimum \(T^*\) such that \(F > 1 - \varepsilon\)
  • Thompson (2024): 890 μs production moves. Our OC: 53 μs at \(D=3\), verified.
HARMONIQS Two knobs (\(D\), \(T\)), one objective: \(|c_0(T)|^2 \to 1\)
2 · The lab-frame problem

The Schrödinger equation becomes highly oscillatory at speed

Lab-frame Hamiltonian:
$$ i\,\partial_t\psi = \Big[-\partial_x^2 + A(t)\,V_0\!\big(x-r(t)\big)\Big]\,\psi $$
The momentum cost:
$$ k_{\max} \sim \frac{m\,\dot r}{\hbar} \quad\to\quad N_x \sim \frac{m\,v_{\rm peak}\,L}{\pi\hbar} $$
  • At \(v_{\rm peak} = 0.17\,\mu\)m/μs: \(k_{\max}\!\approx\!10^4\,\sigma_\psi^{-1}\) — the grid must track the bulk translation
  • Requires \(N_x \sim 5000\) in the lab frame — prohibitive for OC iteration
  • The oscillation is gauge — the physics is in the residual, not the bulk
HARMONIQS Lab frame: \(k\) grows with velocity. The grid pays for motion that carries no physics.
3 · The comoving frame

Ride the trap — velocity is pure gauge, only acceleration couples

Lab frame
physical coordinates — what the hardware moves
\( i\,\partial_t\psi = \big[-\partial_x^2 + A(t)\,V_0\!\big(x-r(t)\big)\big]\,\psi \)
  • the control \(r(t)\) sits inside the Gaussian → nonlinear
  • the trap drags the atom across the grid → large \(k\)
Galilean boost
\(\xi=x-r(t)\), then match the velocity
\( \psi_{\rm lab} = e^{\,i\left(\frac{\dot r}{2}\xi\right)}\,\chi(\xi,t) \)
  • ride the trap's position, then cancel its velocity
  • the velocity coupling \(-\dot r\,p\) cancels exactly
Comoving frame
what the optimizer actually sees
\( i\,\partial_t\chi = \big[-\partial_\xi^2 + A\,V_0(\xi) + \tfrac12\,\ddot r\,\xi\big]\,\chi \)
  • a static well + an inertial tilt \(\propto\ddot r\)
  • \(k\) stays near \(1/\sigma_\psi\) — set by the wavepacket, not the speed
  • dimensionless units: \(\hbar=1,\,2m=1\)
HARMONIQS Constant velocity = free. The comoving frame makes the grid 100× smaller.
4 · The propagator

Strang split-operator: cheap steps on a small grid

½ kinetic kick
diagonal in \(k\) — via FFT
\( e^{-i\,k^2\,\Delta t/2} \)
  • multiply by a phase in momentum space
  • \(k_{\max} = \pi/\Delta x\) — small in comoving!
Full potential kick
diagonal in \(\xi\) — one pointwise multiply
\( e^{-i\,V(\xi,t)\,\Delta t} \)
  • sampled at the midpoint — no matrix assembly
  • \(V = A\,V_0(\xi) + \tfrac12\ddot r\,\xi\)
The payoff
why comoving + Strang = fast OC
\( O(N\log N) \text{ per step, unitary to } 10^{-10} \)
  • comoving \(k\) is small → \(N_x\) drops dramatically
  • \(\Delta t\) is set by the potential, not bulk momentum
  • resolves all modes (bound + continuum) — ungameable
HARMONIQS Comoving + Strang: far smaller grids, \(O(N\log N)\) steps, exact gradients via adjoint — the fast-iteration engine.
5 · SLM transport

Single trap — spectral decomposition and IVP

$$\chi(\xi, t) = \sum_{n=0}^{N_b} c_n(t)\,\phi_n(\xi) \;+\; \int_0^\infty c(E,t)\,\phi_E(\xi)\,dE$$
Spectral decomposition in eigenstates of static well \(H_0 = p^2\!/(2m) + V_0\).
$$H(t) = H_0 + m\ddot{r}(t)\,\xi$$
IVP: atom starts in ground state, \(\mathbf{c}(0) = (1, 0, 0, \ldots)^T\). Project onto \(\phi_m\):
$$i\hbar\,\dot{c}_m = E_m\, c_m + m\ddot{r}(t)\sum_n X_{mn}\, c_n$$
HARMONIQS \(X_{mn} = \langle\phi_m|\xi|\phi_n\rangle\).  Fidelity: \(F = |c_0(T)|^2\).  Goal: find \(r(t)\) with \(\mathbf{c}(T) \approx \mathbf{c}(0)\).
6 · Shortcuts to adiabaticity

OC exploits cancellation of excitations — not avoidance

From slide 5, the exact ODE in the interaction picture (factor out \(e^{-iE_m t/\hbar}\)):
$$\dot{\tilde{c}}_m = \frac{m}{i\hbar}\,\ddot{r}(t)\sum_n X_{mn}\,e^{i\omega_{mn}t}\,\tilde{c}_n$$
First-order approximation: assume \(\tilde{c}_n \approx \delta_{n0}\) (ground state dominates). Then for \(m \neq 0\):
$$\tilde{c}_m(T) \approx \frac{m\,X_{m0}}{i\hbar}\int_0^T \ddot{r}(t)\,e^{i\omega_{m0}\,t}\,dt$$
This is exact at first order: excitation amplitude = Fourier transform of the acceleration, sampled at the transition frequency \(\omega_{m0} = (E_m - E_0)/\hbar\).
Adiabatic: keep \(\ddot{r}\) small so \(|\tilde{c}_m| \ll 1\) always. STA/OC: allow large \(\ddot{r}\), but choose \(r(t)\) so that these integrals vanish at \(T\) for all \(m\) — destructive interference.
HARMONIQS Excitation = \(\widehat{\ddot{r}}(\omega_{m0})\). OC makes this Fourier coefficient vanish at all transition frequencies simultaneously.

7 · AOD transport

Handoff between SLM sites — time-dependent basis

$$H(t) = H_{\rm base}(t) + m\ddot{r}\,\xi$$
where \(H_{\rm base}(t) = p^2/(2m) + V_{\rm mov}(\xi,\eta,t) + V_{\rm src} + V_{\rm dest}\). Tilt-free, has proper bound states.
Expand in eigenstates of \(H_{\rm base}(t)\). These are time-dependent (lensing changes \(V_{\rm mov}\)):
$$i\hbar\,\dot{\tilde{c}}_m = \tilde{E}_m\,\tilde{c}_m + m\ddot{r}\sum_n \tilde{X}_{mn}\,\tilde{c}_n - i\hbar\sum_{n \neq m}\frac{\langle\tilde\phi_m|\partial_t H_{\rm base}|\tilde\phi_n\rangle}{\tilde{E}_n - \tilde{E}_m}\,\tilde{c}_n$$
Two couplings beyond diagonal: tilt (same \(m\ddot{r}\,X\) as SLM) + shape change (\(\partial_t H_{\rm base}\) from lensing). The shape-change term has no SLM analogue — it arises because the trap deforms during transport.
Lensing: \(\sigma = \kappa|\dot{r}|/w_0\), \(\;w_x = w_0\sqrt{1+\sigma^2}\), \(\;A_{\rm eff} = A/\sqrt{1+\sigma^2}\).
HARMONIQS Time-dependent basis, 2D coupled. OC shapes both \(r(t)\) and \(A(t)\). Richer landscape — more drives, more constraints.
7b · AOD shape-change drives

Lensing introduces depth, width, and site-shift couplings

The non-adiabatic coupling \(\langle\tilde\phi_m|\partial_t H_{\rm base}|\tilde\phi_n\rangle\) decomposes into three physical drives:
$$\partial_t H_{\rm base} = \underbrace{\dot{A}_{\rm eff}\,G}_{\text{depth}} + \underbrace{A_{\rm eff}\frac{4\xi^2}{w_x^3}\dot{w}_x\,G}_{\text{width}} + \underbrace{\dot{r}\;\partial_r(V_{\rm src} + V_{\rm dest})}_{\text{site shift}}$$

Depth (\(\dot{A}_{\rm eff} \propto \ddot{r}\) via lensing): breathing mode, couples \(0 \leftrightarrow 2\).

Width (\(\dot{w}_x \propto \ddot{r}\)): operator \(\xi^2\), anisotropic — couples x and y.

Site shift (\(\dot{r}\)): source/dest gradient in comoving frame — the handoff channel.

\(G = e^{-2\xi^2/w_x^2 - 2\eta^2/w_0^2}\). All three vanish for SLM (no lensing, no handoff). This is why the AOD problem is fundamentally 2D and coupled.
HARMONIQS SLM: one drive (\(\ddot{r}\xi\)). AOD: four drives, x–y coupled, time-dependent basis. The numerical cost reflects this.
8 · The grid-honesty problem

Fast moves still excite high \(k\) — penalize it to guarantee transfer

The problem: STA excites higher modes mid-path
  • Faster \(T^*\) → higher transient \(|c_n|^2\) → broader \(|\hat\psi(k)|^2\)
  • If \(k\) exceeds \(k_{\rm Nyq}/3\), the coarse grid aliases — the optimizer exploits this silently
The fix: penalize spectral weight above the grid ceiling
$$ J_{\rm total} = J_{\rm fidelity} + \lambda_p \cdot \frac{L}{N^2}\!\!\sum_{|k|>k_{\rm grid}}\!\! |\hat\psi(k,t)|^2 $$
  • Without penalty: coarse-grid fidelity is wrong (aliasing) — fine-grid rescore disagrees
  • With penalty: coarse → fine transfer with < 0.5% gap
  • The constraint makes the coarse solution calibratable by construction
HARMONIQS Spectral penalty = sufficient condition for grid honesty. A coarse solution that passes it will transfer to any finer grid.
9 · What limits speed

OC beats adiabaticity — hardware sets the real ceiling

$$T_{\rm OC}^* \;\ll\; T_{\rm adiabatic}$$
Trap force (SLM)
hard physical floor
\( m\ddot{r} < \dfrac{2A_0\sqrt{2/e}}{w_0} \)
  • tilt exceeds restoring force → barrier gone, atom escapes
  • no OC can recover
  • sets absolute \(T_{\rm min}\) for single-trap
Lensing (AOD)
velocity-dependent depth loss
\( A_{\rm eff} = \dfrac{A}{\sqrt{1+\sigma^2}} \)
  • fast moves reduce confinement
  • \(A(t)\) compensates, bounded by max power
  • not applicable to SLM
Control bandwidth
hardware ceiling
\( \text{AOD: 50 MHz} \quad \text{SLM: 16 MHz} \)
  • limits how fast \(r(t)\) can change
  • constrains the OC trajectory space
  • goal: find fastest transport under these constraints
HARMONIQS OC lives below the adiabatic knee. The speed frontier is set by hardware (force, lensing, bandwidth), not by adiabaticity.
10 · OC vs min-jerk

AOD handoff: OC vs the best analytic trajectory

Min-jerk (analytic)
Flash–Hogan quintic, min \(\int\dddot r^2\,dt\)
  • \(r(s) = D(10s^3 - 15s^4 + 6s^5)\)
  • single-parameter family — no optimization
  • the best you can do without OC
OC (ours)
B-spline \(r(t)\), adjoint-optimized
  • shaped acceleration uses STA — excites then refocuses
  • spectrally constrained → transfers to fine grid
  • solves on coarse grid, validates on fine
  • At the same \(T\): OC arrives cold where min-jerk arrives hot
  • At the same \(F\): OC achieves it in far less time
HARMONIQS OC shapes what the quintic cannot. Same hardware, same distance — dramatically less heating.
11 · The 10 MHz SLM

Wei et al. RIPA eliminates lensing and handoff entirely

AOD transport (current)
  • Lensing: trap blooms at speed, depth drops
  • Handoff: atom transfers between source → moving → dest
  • 2D coupling: \(w_x \neq w_y\) during transport
  • Numerics: full 2D grid, time-dependent basis
  • 4 drive channels to control simultaneously
SLM transport (10 MHz RIPA)
  • No lensing: trap maintains full depth at all speeds
  • No handoff: single trap, atom always confined
  • x, y decouple: isotropic well always
  • Numerics: 1D eigenbasis ODE (fast, adjoint trivial)
  • 1 drive channel: trajectory \(r(t)\) only
Wei et al. (arXiv:2601.08906, Schuster & Simon): 44 ns rise time, continuous 2D beam motion, 16 MHz per-channel bandwidth. Eliminates both the physical problem (lensing, handoff heating) and the numerical complexity (2D coupled grid → 1D separable ODE).
HARMONIQS SLM removes two problems at once: the physics is simpler (no lensing, no handoff) AND the numerics are faster (1D eigenbasis, analytic adjoint).

13 · From simulation to hardware

The calibration loop: QILC closes the model–truth gap

The pipeline:
1. Solve
coarse grid + spectral penalty
  • fast OC with adjoint gradients
  • spectrally constrained → honest
2. Transfer
coarse → fine validated
  • fine grid = "digital twin" of hardware
  • < 0.5% gap guaranteed
3. Calibrate
QILC on hardware / digital twin
  • measure → response matrix → update
  • converges in ~10 iterations
Why start calibratable: bending energy predicts hardware fidelity
  • Among equal-simulated-\(F\) pulses: bending energy \(\int u''(t)^2\,dt\) correlates with hardware fidelity (Spearman \(\rho = 0.79\))
  • The spectral penalty + jerk regularization produce low-bending-energy pulses by construction — ideal seeds for QILC
QILC Loop
\(\vec u_0\) (OC solution)
↓
Perturb: \(\vec u + \delta\vec u_j\)
↓
Measure: \(y_j = \bar n(\vec u + \delta\vec u_j)\)
↓
Response: \(R_{ij} = \partial y_i / \partial u_j\)
↓
Update: \(\vec u \leftarrow \vec u - \alpha\,R^\top\,\vec y\)
PCA: top 2/25 modes = 90% of curvature
HARMONIQS Smooth pulses (low bending energy) + QILC = hardware-ready in 10 iterations.
14 · Measuring fidelity on hardware

Sideband spectroscopy gives \(\bar n\) — the true arrive-cold metric

After transport, probe motional sidebands:
$$ R = \frac{P_{\rm red}}{P_{\rm blue}} = \frac{\bar n}{1 + \bar n} \quad\Rightarrow\quad F = P(n\!=\!0) = \frac{1}{1+\bar n} $$
  • Survival (most papers): atom still in trap? Not fidelity — heated atom survives too.
  • Sideband ratio (Kaufman/JILA, Thompson): red/blue asymmetry → \(\bar n\) per axis. This IS fidelity.
  • Bell-state parity (Bluvstein/Harvard): operational fidelity — conflates all error sources.
  • Our QILC targets sideband spectroscopy — the only measurement that gives \(|c_0|^2\) directly for motional state.
What labs measure
Harvard (Bluvstein): Bell parity
JILA (Kaufman): Raman sideband → \(\bar n\)
Thompson: Clock + sideband
Browaeys: Survival only (≠ fidelity)
Quantinuum (ions): Carrier Rabi → \(|c_0|^2\)
HARMONIQS Most groups report survival. True fidelity requires sideband spectroscopy — that's what our QILC targets.

16 · Summary & outlook

The pipeline — fast OC, then calibrate to hardware

  • Comoving frame removes bulk oscillation — \(k\) is set by the wavepacket (\(\sim\!100\times\) smaller grid)
  • Strang splitting resolves all modes including continuum — ungameable, \(O(N\log N)\)
  • Spectral penalty guarantees coarse→fine transfer (< 0.5% gap) — calibratable by construction
  • OC as STA — transient excitation (\(c_n\) slosh) refocuses at \(T^*\); beats the adiabatic threshold
  • Lensing adjoint — \(A(t)\) shapes the trap under bloom; 3-drive coupling for handoff (depth + width + jerk)
  • QILC — closes the model–hardware gap using motional sideband measurements
HARMONIQS Fast iteration in 1D → optimize under hardware constraints → extend to 2D lensing + handoff → calibrate to hardware.
