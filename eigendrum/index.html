What my undergrad thesis did

In chapter 6 of my undergrad thesis I put the shape inside a box and built its modes out of the box’s sine waves. Everything outside the shape gets a large penalty V₀. The integrals over each pixel only have to be computed once, so changing the shape is cheap.

H_nm = λ_n δ_nm + V₀ · (sum of pixel integrals outside the shape) eig(H) → the drum’s modes, as V₀ → ∞

The downside is a large table, a dense matrix, and an error that only shrinks like 1/√V₀.

Pixel shapes, done faster

  • Let V₀ go all the way to infinity. The pixels outside the shape then drop out of the problem.
  • Use the pixels themselves as the building blocks. What remains is a sparse matrix with five entries per row.
  • Only ask for the lowest modes, and start from the last answer while you draw. A brush stroke usually takes a few milliseconds.

The “vs undergrad thesis” tab runs both methods side by side.

Smooth shapes

  • Round shapes use Bessel functions: the disk, quarter disk, semicircle and annulus.
  • Three triangles (equilateral, right isosceles and 30-60-90) have exact answers built from sums of plane waves, a trick that goes back to Lamé.
  • The other shapes from my undergrad thesis have no formula. They are solved with finite elements on a mesh that follows the real edge.

Either way the edge is drawn as a smooth curve, not as pixel steps.

Sound and heat

  • A hit is a small bump of width σ. Mode k is excited in proportion to e−λkσ²/2, so a wider hammer reaches fewer high modes and sounds darker. This is the dashed curve on the chart. Modes past λ = 18.4/σ² are below −80 dB and are left out.
  • Exact shapes add up every mode the hit reaches, often more than a thousand. Pixel and mesh shapes use the modes on screen plus a Lanczos estimate of the rest, so no frequencies are left out.
  • Across the room is what a microphone a few meters away hears. Sound comes from the air the drum pushes, so modes whose up and down halves cancel are almost silent. You hear fewer notes and a sharper attack.
  • Contact mic is a sensor stuck to the drum where you hit it. It picks up every mode the hit excites, weighted toward the low ones, so it sounds darker and fuller.
  • For heat, the edges stay at 0° and each source stays at its own temperature. “Steady state” jumps straight to where the temperature settles.
  • The Schrödinger tab solves iψt = −Δψ for a particle trapped in the shape. A wave packet moves, spreads, bounces off the edges and interferes with itself. A single mode just turns its phase, so its |ψ|² never changes.
