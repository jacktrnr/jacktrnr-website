Research

My research centers on the spectral theory of differential operators and its applications — understanding how the spectrum of a linear operator controls the existence, stability, and dynamics of physical states, and building the numerical solvers that make that understanding computable.

Nonlinear waves and scattering resonances. My PhD dissertation (Columbia, 2026; advised by Michael I. Weinstein) establishes a new mechanism for coherent structure formation in nonlinear Schrödinger / Gross–Pitaevskii equations. Classical bifurcation theory constructs localized bound states from L2 eigenvalues of the linear potential. We showed that bound states can also bifurcate from the continuous spectrum, at energies where the linear operator has scattering resonances — poles of the analytically continued reflection coefficient on iℝ. These resonance-induced states emerge at a positive mass threshold and are orbitally stable, with stability decided in closed form from resonance functionals via the Grillakis–Shatah–Strauss variational framework. A companion paper extends the stability and instability analysis to general focusing nonlinearities. Supported by the NSF and the Simons Foundation.

Spectral geometry. At Utah I worked on eigenvalue optimization: choosing a flat torus (a quotient of ℝn by a lattice) that maximizes its Laplacian eigenvalues under a volume constraint, connecting extremal lattice configurations to classical sphere-packing problems (SIAM J. Appl. Algebra Geom., 2023). Separately, I developed a mesh-free spectral expansion method for the Laplace–Beltrami operator on curved manifolds, reducing the eigenproblem to a structured matrix pencil with provable convergence.

Plasma stability. At Zap Energy I built an eigenvalue solver in Julia that tracks embedded eigenvalues through bifurcations via predictor-corrector continuation with arclength parameterization, achieving a 720× speedup over the existing pipeline. This solver enabled a spectral analysis of the sheared-flow Z pinch in the complex-frequency plane, where the analytic dispersion function reveals how discrete modes emerge from and interact with the flow-shifted continuous spectrum — identifying a new class of shear-driven instabilities that dominate the ideal MHD spectrum.

Quantum optimal control. At Harmoniqs I design control protocols for neutral-atom and trapped-ion quantum hardware — optimal atom transport in optical tweezers (minimizing motional excitation under realistic device models) and entangling-gate synthesis during ion transport in Penning micro-traps, using exact configuration-space PDEs with full Coulomb interactions.

2026 · Preprint

On the ideal stability of the sheared-flow Z pinch

D. Crews & J. Turner

Sheared-flow Z-pinch stability has been studied in ideal MHD mostly through growth rates, which suggest that even trans-Alfvénic flow fails to suppress the kink. We instead compute the analytic dispersion function in the complex-frequency plane, where regularization splits it into adiabatic and resonant parts that show how discrete modes emerge from and interact with the continuous spectrum. The Doppler-shifted flow continuum overlaps the interchange branch at all wavenumbers, so even sub-Alfvénic shear stabilizes profiles modestly past the interchange threshold; the kink is shielded by a frequency gap, and trans-Alfvénic flow is needed to shift the continuum into resonance with it — a geometric picture of the stabilization threshold. Shear-driven modes (reflection modes and an acoustic kink) appear at that same threshold and dominate the ideal spectrum, so the ultimate stability of the sheared-flow Z pinch rests on non-ideal physics.

2025 · Preprint

Resonance-induced Nonlinear Bound States

J. Turner & M. I. Weinstein

We study how localized, time-periodic bound states of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation can bifurcate directly from the continuous spectrum at energies associated with scattering and transmission resonances of the underlying linear potential. Rather than requiring a pre-existing linear eigenvalue, these nonlinear bound states are induced by the resonance itself: the nonlinearity converts a long-lived scattering state into a genuinely localized excitation. We characterize the bifurcation, the resulting soliton profiles, and their dependence on the resonance structure — connecting classical scattering theory to the existence of coherent nonlinear waves.

2023 · Journal

Flat Tori with Large Laplacian Eigenvalues

SIAM Journal on Applied Algebra and Geometry

This work studies the shape optimization problem of choosing a flat torus — a quotient of Euclidean space by a lattice — that maximizes its Laplace eigenvalues under a volume constraint. We analyze how the optimal lattice geometry changes with dimension, construct and certify optimizers in dimensions up to eight, and relate the extremal configurations to classical lattice-packing problems. The results give sharp, explicit examples in the geometry of the Laplacian spectrum.

2022 · Preprint

A Generalized Expansion Method for Computing Laplace–Beltrami Eigenfunctions

Numerical spectral geometry on manifolds

We present a spectral expansion method for computing eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator on curved domains and manifolds. By expanding in a well-chosen global basis and reducing the eigenproblem to a structured matrix pencil, the method achieves high accuracy for smooth geometries while avoiding the mesh-generation overhead of finite-element approaches. We demonstrate convergence and accuracy across a range of model geometries.

2021 · Thesis

Expansion Method for Eigenvalue Problems

Honors Thesis · University of Utah

My undergraduate honors thesis introduces the expansion-based approach to Laplacian eigenvalue problems that later grew into the Laplace–Beltrami work above. It develops the method from first principles, establishes its accuracy on canonical domains, and lays out the numerical foundations for computing eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator.
