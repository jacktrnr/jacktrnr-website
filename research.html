Research

My research centers on the spectral theory of differential operators and its applications — understanding how the spectrum of a linear operator controls the existence, stability, and dynamics of physical states, and building the numerical solvers that make that understanding computable.

Nonlinear waves and scattering resonances

My PhD dissertation (Columbia, 2026; advised by Michael I. Weinstein) establishes a new mechanism for coherent structure formation in nonlinear Schrödinger / Gross–Pitaevskii equations. Classical bifurcation theory (Rose–Weinstein) constructs localized bound states from L2 eigenvalues of the linear potential. We showed that bound states can also bifurcate from the continuous spectrum, at energies where the linear operator has scattering resonances — poles of the analytically continued reflection coefficient on iℝ. These resonance-induced states emerge at a positive mass threshold and are orbitally stable, with stability decided in closed form from resonance functionals via the Grillakis–Shatah–Strauss variational framework.

A companion paper extends the stability and instability analysis to general focusing nonlinearities, establishing sharp criteria that determine orbital stability from the slope of the mass-energy curve at the bifurcation point. Both papers are joint with M. I. Weinstein. Supported by the NSF and the Simons Foundation.

Spectral geometry

At Utah I worked on eigenvalue optimization: choosing a flat torus (a quotient of ℝn by a lattice) that maximizes its Laplacian eigenvalues under a volume constraint, connecting extremal lattice configurations to classical sphere-packing problems (SIAM J. Appl. Algebra Geom., 2023, with C.-Y. Kao and B. Osting). Separately, I developed a mesh-free spectral expansion method for the Laplace–Beltrami operator on curved manifolds, reducing the eigenproblem to a structured matrix pencil with provable convergence (with E. Cherkaev and D. Wang).

Plasma stability and MHD

At Zap Energy I built an eigenvalue solver in Julia that tracks embedded eigenvalues through bifurcations via predictor-corrector continuation with arclength parameterization, achieving a 720× speedup over the existing pipeline. This solver enabled a spectral analysis of the sheared-flow Z pinch in the complex-frequency plane, where the analytic dispersion function reveals how discrete modes emerge from and interact with the flow-shifted continuous spectrum — identifying a new class of shear-driven instabilities that dominate the ideal MHD spectrum (with D. Crews; arXiv:2608.00291).

Quantum optimal control

At Harmoniqs I design control protocols for neutral-atom and trapped-ion quantum hardware, working on problems in atom transport, optical tweezer manipulation, and entangling-gate synthesis. The work involves building exact physical models of quantum systems, formulating optimal control problems against those models, and developing Julia software for simulation and optimization.
